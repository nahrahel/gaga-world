# Gaga vocabulary research

Text research for the Gaga world. Confidence: **A** = official Gaga/Batsheva source or Naharin himself; **B** = 2+ independent secondary sources; **C** = single source; **D** = unverified or folklore. Source codes resolve in section 7. Galili wrote G08, G09a, G09b, GP2, NG and G15, so those count as one voice for independence. Researched 2026-10-29.

## 1. Core principles

| Principle | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Listen to the body first, then direct it | A | Official work instruction: listen to the body and sense its range before telling it what to do (GP1). Naharin describes choreography as "the act of listening to your body before you tell it what to do" (GM). |
| No mirrors | A | Naharin's own text says there are no mirrors (N08); a senior teacher recalls the shift from looks to feeling (GP4). Batsheva studios have none; elsewhere mirrors are covered (G08, Q16, W24). |
| No observers, no latecomers | B (observers), A (latecomers) | Official page: no late entry (GP1). Observers forbidden per HG, Q16, JTA, SN, H15; dancers also may not stop to watch (DGa). |
| Continuous, never stops | A | Official work instruction: work continuously (GP1). Older official wording: one session, no pauses or separate exercises; when tired, drop to 30% or 20%, float, or rest without losing awakened sensations (V16 quoting gagapeople). |
| Instructions layer, never replace | A | GP1; each task stacks on earlier ones (G08, Q16). |
| Teacher dances along | B, plus A for taking inspiration from the teacher | Teacher moves the whole hour (H15); dances alongside students (Q16); leads from the room's center, demonstrating (HA); students told to watch the teacher (SM). |
| Pleasure, especially during effort | A | GP1, N08. Effort is distinct from pain; "The only pain welcome in Gaga is the burning sensation in our muscles" (GP1). |
| Available | A | Naharin's text ends with becoming available (N08); availability described as near-animal readiness (GP2). Coined word: dolfi. |
| Yielding to gravity | A, but needs nuance | Naharin: yielding is constant while ready to snap (V16 quoting the official text). Float means sensing weight without letting gravity define form (Q16, WP). Collapse is used to measure the force that opposes gravity (GM). Depict yielding plus lift, never slump. |
| Delicacy and explosive power | A | GP1, GP3 (delicacy even inside explosive moments), N08. |
| Silliness, humor, laughing at oneself | A | GP1 (Gaga/dancers: value in silliness), N08, GP3. |
| Groove, with or without music | A | N08, GP2. |
| Sensation and imagery over shapes | A | Imagery is rich but the work is physical (GP1); emphasis on how over what (GP2); participants explore qualities with no target shape (H15). |
| Added: eyes open, aware of others, not the center | A | GP1, N08. Instructors insist eyes stay open (SM). |
| Added: work quietly; voice only when instructed | A | GP1. |
| Added: plenty of time, especially when moving fast | A | N08; cue "move like you have plenty of time" (HG). |
| Added: multi-layer tasks; slow travel with quick action inside | A | N08. |
| Added: understatement and exaggeration; efficient force; "other" forces | A | N08, GP2. |
| Added: calm and alert at once; change movement habits | A | N08. |

## 2. Glossary

Visible-movement cells use Laban qualities from H15 where cited; everything else is an animation translation marked (inf.). Abbreviations: Init = initiation point.

### 2a. Coined Gaga lexicon (Naharin's invented words)

About 32 coined words exist (H15). Beginners hear few of them: at Suzanne Dellal, H15 heard mainly lena, dolfi and pika, and Q16 says open-class teachers favor imagery over lexicon. Moons, lava and oba are the coined or semi-coined terms Q16 heard even with new students. Some are named after people (lena after a rehearsal director's daughter, dolfi after Naharin's gardener: H15, RS); some echo Japanese (Q16).

| Term | Meaning | Imagery / cue | Region | Visible movement | Conf | Refs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lena | Movement engine between navel and groin; moving from it as prime mover | Engine low in the belly; teachers sometimes call it center | Lower belly, pelvis | Init pelvis; spine and limbs follow with lag; any tempo; mid to large; continuous; grounded, soft knees (inf.) | A | NG, Q16, H15, G15 |
| Biba | Stretching the body away from the sit bones, making space in the low spine | Recorded cue: move everything away from the sit bones | Sit bones, lumbar spine | Init sacrum; torso lengthens up while pelvis stays heavy; slow, small, sustained (inf.) | A | NG, H15, SN, WP |

| Pika (+ yoyo) | Pika: area between groin and rectum (one source: just under the pubic bone). Yoyo (from 2013): pika engaged with the low belly | Rarely explained to beginners | Pelvic floor | Nearly invisible; slight pelvic lift or tuck (inf.) | A term; B location (sources differ) | GP2, H15, W24, WP, G15 |

## 3. Class arc (Gaga/people, about 60 minutes)

No fixed structure exists: teachers do not plan in advance, but one teacher lists required ingredients as "effort has to be there, speed, letting go, countdowns, sensitivity and layers" (teacher B in H15). Each session centers on 1 or 2 main concepts that get layered (Q16). Phase timings below are an inference for scripting; no source gives minutes.

1. **Before start (0 to 2 min).** Doors close; no late entry (GP1). Teacher asks if anyone is new and states the rules: keep moving, quiet, eyes open (H15).
2. **Opening (about 0 to 10).** Standing (G08). Float first, nearly always (Q16, H15, W24). Weight shifts side to side, letting movement travel (G08); walking and stretching in Naharin's own class (G09a). Waking parts in turn: core, arms, legs, fingers, toes, head (DS); lunas and biba appear early in a recorded class (H15).
3. **Building (about 10 to 35).** New layers stack: spine, flesh, traveling balls, curves, textures (soft, thick, honey), shake and quake, then combinations such as slow motion plus shaking, the hardest task in G09a. Teachers occasionally return everyone to float to reset before a new layering (Q16).
4. **Peaks (about 25 to 50).** "Climaxes of effort" then a drop back to small movement with full-body sensation (DS). Small explosions, punching, jumps across the room (HG, Q16). Grooving, laughing while grooving, partner moments (smile to your partner, touching to direct attention) (G09a, G08, H15). Level changes: floor and back, sometimes several times; travel through space (G08, TQ, H15).
5. **Closing (last 5 to 10).** Often a vigorous burst with body slapping and a countdown from 10, then release into float (Q16); or simply more floating (G09a). One account ended with a dance party (JTA). No formal thanks ritual (Q16).

**Cue rate.** Not quantified anywhere. Accounts describe near-continuous guidance like a moving meditation (TQ) and a recorded class full of instructions (H15); a shake can build over several minutes (DGb). Practical model (inf.): one main idea sustained for 2 to 5 minutes, refined by short sub-cues every 10 to 30 seconds.

**How teachers speak.** Short present-tense imperatives and images, often inclusive "let's" (G15 via Q16), calm tone (HG). Paraphrased examples: soften knees and ankles; connect to the seaweed quality of the spine; find small collapses in the chest; softer in the spine, looser in the chest; feel the moons under your feet; float; quake; boil like spaghetti; don't be shy of the effort; enjoy the burning; be silly; give it to someone; ten, nine, eight... (HG, H15, DGa). Coined words are always explained with images for newcomers (H15). Language: mostly English internationally (Q16); Tel Aviv classes have run mostly in Hebrew (G08, G09a).

**Music.** Teacher's own playlist, from ambient sound to techno, pop, samba, jazz, classical; silence used often (Q16, H15). Music may set mood, push energy, or run against the movement quality (H15). Not essential (DI); groove should exist without it (N08).

## 5. Accuracy pitfalls

**Conceptual misconceptions**
- *Gaga as choreography or set steps.* "Naharin's choreography is not Gaga itself, and Gaga is not choreography" (Galili, GP2). No sequences are taught; Naharin calls it an evolving language (GM).
- *Gaga as anything-goes freestyle.* Tasks are specific, terms standardized, and a shared look emerges: one interviewee says almost everyone floats in water the same way, with a fleshy, sinewy, snake-like quality (Q16). Copying the teacher is accepted (kagami).
- *Gaga as a codified technique.* Gaga rejects the label; Q16 calls it a metatechnique layered on dancers' existing training.
- *Gaga as only soft and flowy.* It is a sweaty workout with explosive punches, jumps and countdown peaks (HG, JTA, Q16).
- *Float as limp surrender.* Float opposes gravity lightly; dead flesh is the thing to avoid (W24, Q16).
- *Conflation.* Steve Paxton's "small dance" (CI) is a different practice. Lady Gaga and gaga ball are unrelated. Naharin calls the name itself meaningless (DS).
- *Coined words in every class.* Beginners hear mostly images; coined words are shortcuts used more in intensives (Q16, H15).

**3D depiction risks**
- Mirrors on walls, or anyone watching from the sides. Both are banned. The user's camera is effectively an observer; framing it as a participant's view is closer to the practice (inf.).
- Rows facing a front wall. Accounts place the teacher in the center with students in a loose cluster around (HG, HA).
- Teacher standing still and talking. The teacher dances the whole time (H15, Q16).
- Unison. Everyone answers the same cue at once, but timing, amplitude and shape differ per person; resemblance is loose (Q16, G09b).
- Stopping between exercises or idling in a neutral stance. Idle must be float or reduced-volume motion; Gaga never resets to stillness (GP1).
- Presentational faces or performing outward. Teachers correct performing for an audience (G09b). Faces are active and playful, eyes open, sometimes half-closed with mouth open (Q16). Faceless figures can carry this through head, jaw and neck motion (inf.).
- Eyes closed, trance look. The rule is eyes open.
- Grimacing effort. Effort is high but linked to pleasure; pain is out.
- Perfect ballet alignment. Torsos release, arch, go asymmetric; bent knees and deep plié dominate; much floor work (Q16 interviewee).
- Dance clothing. Barefoot or socks, loose comfortable clothes (GP1, Q16).
- Silence or only ambient music. Music varies widely, including club beats and silence.
- **Naming and licensing.** Gaga teaching is limited to certified teachers, and a 2015 intensive waiver barred "Gaga-inspired" classes (Q16). Consider permission before branding the product "Gaga".

## 6. Gaga/people vs Gaga/dancers

| Aspect | Gaga (Gaga/people) | Gaga/dancers |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Ages 16+, no dance background; first-timers beside veterans (GP1, H15) | Professional and advanced student dancers 16+; Batsheva's daily training (GP1, Q16) |
| Length | About 60 min (GP1) | 60 to 75 min (GP1); often 75 (DI, H15) |
| Content | Same principles and language; sensations such as float, shake, quake, lengthen, sink, release (Q16) | Same base plus abstracted ballet work (tendu, plié, penché, arabesque) called by name; repertory in intensives (Q16, GP2, DI) |
| Range | Wide but self-paced; intensity scaled by the person (30% rule) | Extreme range; rapid switch from hyper-delicate to hyper-aggressive; explosive power and texture control (GP1, Q16) |
| Floor work | Present (level changes) | Present, plus jumps and repertory phrases |
| Other tracks | Gaga/seated (seated, Israel), Gaga/families (ages 5 to 9 with parents), Gaga Online | GagaLabs, workshops |

Visual implication (inf.): a people class shows mixed bodies and moderate amplitude; a dancers class shows larger extensions, faster switches, recognizable ballet shapes melted by Gaga textures.
